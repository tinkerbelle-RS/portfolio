01 Why AIUX

AIUX sits where I already spend my time: making capable models legible and steerable in real interfaces, while respecting compliance, safety, and diverse user intuition. At Accenture I owned the full user-facing layer for Gemini applications: how people prompt, how outputs appear, and how the UI hides complexity without hiding accountability.

I am drawn to experimental prototyping as a way to test hypotheses about mental models, error recovery, and guardrails that feel human-centered rather than bolted on. Across healthcare compliance tools, enterprise LLM applications, and public-sector identity systems, I have consistently worked at the same edge: how do you design an AI experience that respects what users intuitively want to do while holding firm on what the system cannot allow? My trajectory, spanning security operations, policy-facing research, and on-device product UX for regulated domains, grounds that question in how systems actually fail and how organizations govern them.

03.5 Case studies

CITRIS Fellowship · California Dept. of Technology · Jan 2026 to Present

Identity systems & the humans who navigate them

Most identity governance frameworks are written for administrators, not users. My fellowship research examines how the UI layer, the moment where policy becomes human experience, consistently fails the people it is meant to protect, particularly in under-resourced organizations. As AI becomes embedded in identity workflows for fraud detection, access control, and eligibility, the design of those systems determines whether people can participate in or contest decisions made about them.

Compliance Gateway · San Francisco, CA · Jan to Aug 2024

Designing creative freedom inside compliance constraints

Healthcare professionals and enterprise users don't want a form, they want to explore, ask questions in natural language, and get useful outputs. But they cannot accidentally input protected health information or produce outputs that contradict clinical procedures. The hardest design problem wasn't security, it was making users feel expressive freedom while quietly enforcing domain rules behind the scenes. I built scaffolded prompting flows that guided compliant inputs without feeling prescriptive, designed guardrail responses in plain language rather than generic errors, and created role-aware interaction layers so healthcare and enterprise users each got an experience native to their domain.

Accenture · San Francisco, CA · May 2024 to Aug 2025

Building the interaction layer of a Gemini application

The core design challenge was that different users needed fundamentally different experiences from the same underlying model. Users working with private data and fixed compliance templates needed a constrained, structured interface, because too much freedom created security risk and compliance violations. Users working with public-facing data needed the opposite: expressive latitude and minimal friction. Making the right call for each context required user interviews across multiple client teams, translating what users actually wanted from the system into interface decisions about prompting structure, defaults, and guardrail visibility. The design was never one-size-fits-all, it was a set of deliberate, research-informed tradeoffs made context by context.
